# Video Pipeline

The safest route to a consistent Odin film is still-first production. Approve identity and composition in one frame, then animate that frame with controlled motion.

## 1. Design the shot

Define the story beat, location, wardrobe look, framing, lens behavior, light direction, duration, and final delivery aspect ratio. Keep each generated shot short enough to evaluate—typically two to five seconds.

## 2. Build the keyframe

Use the locked identity reference pack plus the scene template. Generate a still at or above delivery resolution. Check face geometry, age, hair, beard, hands, body proportions, garment construction, and signature objects before motion.

## 3. Animate conservatively

Use image-to-video or a reference-capable video model. Ask for one dominant subject action and one camera action. Excess motion creates identity drift. Prefer real-time speed, plausible weight, stable lighting, and restrained facial movement.

## 4. Assemble the film

Edit approved shots outside the generation model. Use sound design, typography, grain, color, and transitions to create energy instead of asking one generation to do everything. Preserve a shot ledger that links every final clip to its source image and generation settings.

## 5. Run continuity QA

Review adjacent frames and adjacent shots at full resolution. The face is necessary but not sufficient: compare shoulder width, hand size, hair length, facial-hair edge, watch wrist, garment closures, pendant, and environmental light direction.

## 6. Publish responsibly

Add fictional-AI disclosure at the account, campaign, or page level. Retain masters and provenance records. Do not upload customer or performer likenesses without explicit rights and consent.

## Web presentation

The campaign page does not re-encode its films. JavaScript calculates progress inside each long scroll section and seeks the original video file to the corresponding timestamp. The chase source remains first; the origin source remains second.
